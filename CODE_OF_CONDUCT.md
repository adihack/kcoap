# Code of conduct

kcoap adopts the **[Contributor Covenant, version 2.1](https://www.contributor-covenant.org/version/2/1/code_of_conduct/)**
as its code of conduct. Everyone who takes part in the project agrees to follow it. Its text is not
copied here; please read it at the link.

The sections below complete it for kcoap: where it applies, how to report a problem, and what happens
after a report.

## Where it applies

- every space the project runs: this repository's issues, pull requests, reviews and commit messages,
  and the website https://kcoap.dev once it is online;
- any channel, event or account that the project runs in the future;
- anywhere a person represents kcoap, for example when speaking for the project at an event or
  posting from a project account.

## How to report

Email **adrian.soltys@seelso.net**, with "kcoap conduct" in the subject. Include what you can:

- what happened, and when;
- where it happened, with links to the comments, issues or pull requests involved;
- who was involved, and whether anyone else saw it;
- whether it is still going on, and whether you feel unsafe.

You do not need to have every detail, and you do not need to be the person it happened to.

kcoap has one maintainer today. If your report is about the maintainer, or you prefer not to write to
them, use GitHub's own reporting instead: the **Report content** item in the menu of a comment, issue
or pull request, or GitHub's report form for a user. GitHub reviews those reports itself.

## Confidentiality

Reports are confidential. The maintainer does not share the name of the person who reported without
their consent, and shares the details of a report only as far as needed to act on it. Records of
reports are kept privately.

## What the maintainer does

1. Confirms receipt within a week, usually sooner.
2. Reviews what happened, and may ask the reporter or others for more information.
3. Hears the person reported, unless that would put someone at risk.
4. Decides on a response, following the Covenant's enforcement guidelines: a private correction, a
   warning, a temporary ban or a permanent ban from the project's spaces. Content that breaks the code
   of conduct may be edited, hidden or removed at any point.
5. Tells the reporter what was decided, and the person reported what applies to them.

Nobody may be punished or treated worse for reporting in good faith. Retaliation against a reporter
is itself a breach of this code.

A person who disagrees with a decision may ask the maintainer to review it once, by email, with any
new information.
